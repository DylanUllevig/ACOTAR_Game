# Chapter 29: Days at the chateau. Elain's garden under Father's window; Nesta under the mulberry tree;
# the purses; the walk to the old cottage, so small now, and the forest that is only a forest.

== ch29_garden
> set_flag ch29_garden
> chapter_card 29
N The tale of my years with Aunt Ripleigh almost told itself. I read to her every day. She taught me how a lady sits and walks and speaks, from her pillows. I nursed her until she slipped away in her sleep, a fortnight ago, and left me everything she had.
N Everything turned out to be a great deal. The trunks that came with me held more than dresses: gold, and gems so raw and huge they could have bought a thousand estates.
N Father had shut himself away with them in his study, the room whose window looks over Elain's garden. I could see him at his desk, weighing a rough red stone as big as a hen's egg on a little brass scale.
N His eyes were clear again, and he moved like a man with somewhere to be. Even his limp had eased, thanks to a tonic and a salve that a passing healer had pressed on him and refused payment for. I would have loved Tamlin for that kindness alone.
> objective Sit with Elain in her garden.

N This little garden under the window was hers alone. She'd chosen and planted every bulb and shrub, and she pulled her own weeds, though she admitted the servants carried the heavy watering cans. She'd have cried to see the gardens I'd grown used to.

@elain/sad Everyone behaved as if we'd been sick for eight years, or living abroad. As if we hadn't been three villages away, in that cottage. Not one person mentioned it. Not one.
@elain/sad Sometimes I almost miss those years. Even being hungry, and cold. This house is so large, and Father is never free, and Nesta...

@elain/neutral She didn't finish the season. She refuses every invitation now, and when my friends call she just stares at them until they make excuses and go. Maybe you could talk to her?

== ch29_study
> set_flag ch29_study
N One day blurred into the next. The darkness in me stayed put, and I couldn't stomach the idea of picking up a brush. So I spent my hours in Elain's garden while she chattered about each new bud, and about the vegetable bed she meant to dig beside the glasshouse.
N She had come alive in this place. There wasn't a gardener or maid who didn't smile at her; even the fierce old cook kept finding reasons to bring her plates of tarts. The lean years had buried her light, but they hadn't put it out. She was generous, and loving, and kind, and I was proud to call her my sister.
@father/neutral There. Every stone weighed twice and written down. You are a very wealthy young woman, Feyre.
@feyre/neutral Put a little of it into your business. And draw me up some small purses of silver, would you? Four of them.
@father/neutral Purses? Whatever for?
@feyre/neutral A walk.

== ch29_cottage
> set_flag ch29_cottage
N Mud crept up my hem and I didn't care. The wind moved in the trees, the long grass hissed and sighed, and if I let my thoughts drift I could almost believe I was walking beside Tamlin in his woods.
N No word had come. Every night I went to bed hoping to wake in his house, or to find a message calling me back. Worse than the waiting was the fear that crept in under it: that whoever this Amarantha was, she would find a way to hurt him.
N I came round a bend I could have walked in my sleep, and there it was.
N So small. Had it always been so small? Elain's old flower bed had grown into a wild snarl of weeds and blossom. The ward-marks were still cut into the doorstep. Someone had hung a new door where the beast had smashed the old one, but one of the little round window panes had cracked, and inside, it was dark.
> objective Look around the old cottage.

== ch29_forest
> set_flag ch29_forest
N From the door, my old path crossed the road and the long hump of the field to the edge of the trees. I'd walked it every morning, snow or rain. My forest.
N It had seemed so terrible, once. Hungry, and cruel, and full of death. Now it only looked like trees. Plain. Ordinary.

== ch29_cottage_end
> set_flag ch29_cottage_end
N Elain said she missed this place. What did she see in it? Not the cage I'd always seen. A refuge, maybe, in a hard world. She had kept hunting for the good in it all the same, while I thought her a fool for trying.
N She had looked at this house with hope, and I had looked at it with hate. I knew now which of us had been the stronger.

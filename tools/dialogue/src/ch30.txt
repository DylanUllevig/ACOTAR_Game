N One more errand before I went back to my father's house.
N The village that had once looked straight through me now stopped in the road to stare. People I'd sold pelts to for half their worth stepped into my path to ask after my poor aunt, my inheritance, my plans.
N I gave them nothing to chew on. Polite, and brief, and walking. By the time I reached the poor lane at the bottom of the village, where the roofs sagged and the mud never quite dried, I felt as if I'd been skinned.

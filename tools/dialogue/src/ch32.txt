== ch32_tale
> panel tale_emissary
@alis/neutral A hundred years ago, she came to us as an envoy from Hybern. She went from court to court charming every High Lord she met, with talk of trade and friendship between our lands. The flower that never fades, they called her.
@alis/neutral For fifty years she lived among us, a courtier of no court at all. Making amends, she said, for what Hybern did in the War.
N The Suriel had told me of a king across the sea who had never forgiven the Treaty, and who sent his deadliest servants into the other courts to learn who might share his hunger for the human lands.
@feyre/afraid She fought in the War? Against us?
> panel tale_sister
@alis/neutral In the war she was the King of Hybern's finest general. She led from the front, and butchered humans and every faerie who stood with them. Her little sister, Clythia, rode at her side, and was no gentler.
@alis/neutral Then Clythia lost her heart to a mortal. Jurian, the general of the human armies. She slipped away to meet him again and again, too lovesick to see what he was doing: wringing her sister's battle plans out of her, one secret at a time.
@alis/sad Amarantha had her suspicions. But she wouldn't have him killed while it would shatter her sister. She, who liked killing better than anything, held back. Out of love.
@feyre/afraid What happened?
@alis/angry Once he'd taken all he needed from Clythia, he killed her slowly. He staked her with ash so she couldn't fight him, and left what remained where her sister would find it.
@alis/neutral She's hated humans ever since, with a fury you can't imagine. When the Treaty freed the slaves, she butchered hers sooner than let them go.

== ch32_curse
@alis/neutral They knew each other long before. His family was tied to Hybern. In the War, the Spring Court fought on Hybern's side to keep the humans enslaved. His father was a fickle, vicious lord, close to their king, and to Amarantha. Tamlin often went with him to Hybern, as a boy.
N He had told me once that he would fight for anyone's freedom. That he'd never stand for slavery. Was that shame over his family? Or had he learned, somehow, what it was to be a slave?
@alis/neutral She came to want him, with her whole wicked heart. But he knew the stories. What she'd done in the War. What she did to Jurian. He kept his distance and kept out of her bed, right up until she stole his power.
@alis/sad So he sent Lucien to speak for him and ask for terms. She turned them down, and Lucien told her exactly where she could go. For that she took his eye. Dug it out with one fingernail, tore up his face, and sent him back so bloodied that Tamlin retched when he saw him.
N I couldn't let myself picture what Lucien must have looked like, to turn a High Lord's stomach.
> panel tale_masks
@alis/neutral After that, she held a masquerade Under the Mountain. All the courts came. A peace offering, she said, and masks, so Lucien needn't show his ruined face. The whole Spring Court was to come, even the servants, and wear masks in honor of Tamlin's gift for changing his shape.
@alis/neutral He wanted to end it without bloodshed. So he took us. All of us.
@alis/angry In front of the whole court, she offered him peace if he'd be her lover and sit at her side. He refused to let her lay a finger on him. Not after Lucien. He told her he'd rather bed a mortal than her, since even her sister had chosen a mortal over her.
N I winced.
@alis/neutral You can imagine how she took that. Still, she said she'd be kind, and leave him a way out of the curse she put on him. He spat at her. She only laughed.
@alis/neutral Forty-nine years, she allowed him, and then she would come to collect. To end the curse he needed a mortal girl who would take him as her husband. Not any girl. One whose heart was cold toward our kind and full of hate for us. One who would kill a faerie.
N The floor tilted under me. I was glad of the wall at my back.
@alis/angry And the faerie had to be one of his own, sent across the Wall by his own order, to die. She could only be brought here if she killed one of them for no reason but hate. The way Jurian killed Clythia. So he'd feel what she had felt.
@feyre/afraid The Treaty...
@alis/neutral A lie. There's nothing in the Treaty about it. You can kill as many of us as you please and never pay for it. You killed Andras. That day's sacrifice, sent over by Tamlin himself.
N Tamlin had said Andras went searching for a cure. Not for any blight, then. For this.
N The wolf had just watched me, before my arrow found him. He had allowed it, so that all of this could start, so Tamlin would have his chance. And Tamlin had sent him knowing he might not come back. Oh, Tamlin.
@alis/neutral To her it was a joke, and a cruel one. You mortals hate and fear us so deeply that no girl who'd killed one of us out of hate could ever love one of us. Yet that was the only way to end it. She had to tell him she loved him, to his face, and mean every word, before his forty-nine years ran out.
@alis/sad She knows how much you mortals care for a pretty face, so she bound the masks to our faces, and to his, to make it harder still. Then she bound our tongues, so not one of us could say a word about the curse. The lies about the blight were the best any of us could manage.
@alis/neutral That my tongue's free to tell you means she thinks her game is won.
> panel tale_wolves
@alis/sad At first he sent a man over the Wall every day, all in the shape of wolves, so one of you might be likelier to want to kill them. The ones who came back told of girls who screamed and ran and begged, and never raised a hand. The ones who didn't come back, he felt die. Killed by hunters, or old women. Never the right girl.
@alis/sad Two years of that. Choosing, every day, who went over. When there were only a dozen of them left, it broke him, and he stopped.
